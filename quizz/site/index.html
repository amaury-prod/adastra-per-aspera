The 6PM dinner mistake

Marcus, 60-something

For years I ate dinner at six. My wife liked it early. So did I. Then TV, then bed around eleven. And every night, 3AM. Eyes open. Wide awake. I never once connected the two.

Here's the math I finally did. Dinner at six. Bed at eleven. That's five hours before I even lay down. By three, it had been nine hours. Nine hours on an empty tank.

My body wasn't broken. It was hungry. And a hungry body doesn't stay quiet. It sends a wake-up signal. That signal is what popped my eyes open.

One night we ate late. Company came by. We didn't sit down till almost eight. Real food too. Chicken, greens, sweet potato. I went to bed at eleven like always. And I woke up at six-thirty. Straight through.

I thought it was luck. So I tried it again the next night. And the next. It wasn't luck. It was dinner. Nobody had ever told me to look there.

Try this tonight

Marcus's evening drink

This is the drink my grandmother made. Here's the whole recipe. Nothing left out.

  1. Heat a mug of water. Hot, not boiling.
  2. Crush one clove of garlic. Let it sit two minutes.
  3. Drop in the garlic and the juice of half a lemon.
  4. Stir in a spoon of honey. Sip it slow, an hour before bed.

Tastes better than it sounds. The honey does the heavy lifting.

Make it a small ritual. Same mug, same chair, same time. Your body learns the signal.

If you take medication, run it by your doctor first. Then enjoy it.

The brain dump

Put a notebook and a pen by the bed. Tonight, before lights out, open it.

Write the three things turning in your head. The bill. The call you owe. The thing you said to your daughter. Just three. One line each. Then close the notebook.

Here's why it works. Your brain hands you that list at 3AM because it's scared you'll forget. Now it's written. Your brain can let it go.

If you wake up anyway, don't reach for the phone. Reach for the notebook. Add the thought. Close it. Lie back down.

If you still wake up at 3

Some nights you'll wake up anyway. Here's what to do, in order.

  1. Don't look at the clock. Turn it around before bed if you have to. Knowing it's 3:07 only makes it worse.
  2. Breathe. In for four, out for six. Slow out-breath. Do it ten times.
  3. Count backwards from 300 by 3. 300, 297, 294. It's boring on purpose.

Your brain can't worry and count at the same time. Most nights you won't reach 200. If you do, keep going.

Don't get up. Don't turn on the light. Just breathe and count.

Here's what I'd do in your shoes

Five small things between dinner and lights out. Nothing to swallow, nothing to buy at a pharmacy.

  1. Eat dinner later, or make it bigger.A full tank at bedtime means no wake-up signal at 3.
  2. Swap the evening drink.Hot water, garlic, lemon, honey. Not wine, not coffee, not the fancy tea.
  3. One thing from the kitchen after dinner.A spoon of honey or a small banana. Fuel for the small hours.
  4. Cool the room before you get in it.Crack a window at nine. A cool room keeps the hot wake-up away.
  5. Dim the house an hour before bed.Lamps, not ceiling lights. No phone in bed. Bright light tells your body it's noon.

Start with the one that feels easiest. Add one more each night.

What my grandmother knew

Marcus, 60-something

My grandmother never had a sleep problem. Not one I ever heard about. Every evening, same thing. She'd stand at the stove in that little kitchen. A pot of water going. Garlic on the board. A lemon. Honey from a jar with no label.

She'd make her hot drink and sit down. Nobody rushed her. She'd sip it slow while the house went quiet. Then she'd go to bed. And she'd sleep till the rooster.

I asked her once what was in it. She laughed. “Nothing you can't grow, baby.” She used to say God gave us everything we need. She wasn't preaching. She meant the kitchen.

I forgot all of that for forty years. I bought the teas in the fancy boxes. The gummies. The pills.

Then one night I stood at my own stove. Garlic, lemon, honey, hot water. I sat down and sipped it slow. And I thought about her. That was the first night in a long time I slept past three.
